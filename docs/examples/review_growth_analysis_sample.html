Executive Summary

What we found, what we recommend

Top Finding

62% of invite-triggered negative reviews were submitted within 24 hours of the invite — the customer was already unhappy before opening the email. Moving the invite from the current 11–14 days after order to 22–30 days is associated with a negative-review rate of 11% instead of ~19% (a 42% reduction). On top of that, 21% of negative reviewers had a support ticket before their invite was sent — a detectable signal that can suppress those invites automatically.

1 · Diagnosis

Invites go out when problems are at their peak: the 11–14-day window has an 18.8% negative rate. Complaints concentrate on size expectations, customer-service silence, refund friction and one product family.

2 · Recommendation

Ticket-gated, later-window invites + exclusion of high-risk SKUs + template/subject split-tests. Recovery outreach to existing ≤3★ reviewers as a second phase.

3 · Expected impact

~36 fewer negatives per year (−37%), moving the Trustpilot average from 4.29★ towards ~4.45★ with the first two levers alone — with a small, accepted dip in review volume.

Lever A: ticket-gated invites
Detection
Does this customer have a recent ticket in the helpdesk (matched on hashed email / customer key)?
Hold rule
Exclude the order from the invite segment while a recent ticket exists; it re-enters automatically once the ticket ages out of the lookback.
Impact
Would have caught 20 of 97 (21%) negative reviews. False-positive suppression: 10 of 214 (4.7%) positive reviewers also had tickets — minimal bleed.
Lever B: invite at 22–30 days
Current state
Invite at ~11–14 days (p50). Negative-review rate in this window: 18.8%.
Proposed
Send 22–30 days post-order. Observed negative rate in this window: 11.1% — a 42% reduction.
Benefit
Durability issues (stones, chains) surface within 7–14 days. By day 22–30 they are either resolved or accepted.
Tradeoff
Conversion may dip as post-purchase excitement wanes. Monitored weekly with a rollback trigger (see rollout).
Lever C: product-level handling
For the necklace family and other high-risk SKUs the invite is not the root problem — the product experience is. Excluding those buyers from the main invite flow, and sending a proactive care email at day 7 (care guide, sizing guidance, how to request an adjustment), shifts sentiment before any review request arrives and pre-empts the refund friction behind ~10% of negatives.

Rating impact: removing ~36 negatives (avg ~1.8★) from the 12-month mix moves the average from 4.29★ to roughly 4.45★ using Levers A + B alone. Trustpilot's TrustScore also weights recency, so improvements show in the headline score faster than the raw mean suggests.

Lesson from validation — verify suppression rules against live data
The first version of the ticket exclusion matched ticket statuses open and new. The helpdesk integration only ever emits closed, active, pending and spam, so the exclusion silently matched nothing. It was caught when a negative reviewer who had two tickets still received an invite. Switching to an “any ticket, any status” building block, then verifying the customer disappeared from the live segment, shrank the eligible audience by 17% (1,414 → 1,169 customers). We now check every exclusion rule against real rows before activation, and each report re-confirms it with a before/after segment count.

Action Items

  • 1
    Implement ticket-gated invite suppression. Exclude customers with a recent support ticket from the invite segment; re-admit them automatically once the ticket ages out. Based on 12-month data this would have prevented 21% of negative reviews (20/97) with ~4.7% bleed of happy customers. Strongest for tickets that arrived 2–14 days before the invite.
  • 2
    Move the invite to a 22–30-day window. The 22–30-day bucket shows an 11.1% negative rate vs 18.8% at 11–14 days (−42%). Roll out as a phased change, monitor both negative rate and review volume, and keep a rollback trigger (conversion < 1.5% for 3 weeks).
  • 3
    Escalate the personalized-necklace product family for quality review. 23 negative product reviews in 12 months at a 22.3% negative rate (avg 2.0★). Primary complaints: chain fragility, size smaller than shown, mismatch with photos. Update the listing (size reference photo, accurate chain gauge) and review chain sourcing. This SKU likely drives the “size” theme — the largest Trustpilot complaint category (13%).
  • 4
    Add a proactive care touchpoint at day 7 for high-risk SKUs. Care guide, sizing/adjustment process and metal-care tips turn a potential complaint into a brand moment and surface issues early enough to resolve before the day-22+ invite.
  • 5
    Refine the ticket filter to support-intent subjects. The ticket data includes many automated replies. Once the basic gate is validated, restrict suppression to subjects with quality signals (“resiz”, “wrong”, “broke”, “return”, “refund”, “defect”, “problem”, “damage”, “tarnish”, “missing”) to reduce false suppression.
  • 6
    Fix the customer-service response gap. 11 negative TP reviews (11%) cite no response from customer service. Tickets currently active should be triaged within a 24-hour SLA, quality complaints first. Resolving the issue before the invite is the fastest path to prevention — and the prerequisite for the Phase 2 recovery campaign.
  • 7
    Capture the order ID on tickets at ingestion. ~81% of ticket subjects contain a parseable order ID, but only ~3% of tickets have the field populated. Extracting it at import enables order-level ticket→review matching (more precise than email matching alone).
  • 8
    Phase 2: launch a negative-review recovery campaign. Reply to ≤3★ reviewers within 24h, resolve, and invite them to update the review. Recovered reviews move the average faster than new positives.
