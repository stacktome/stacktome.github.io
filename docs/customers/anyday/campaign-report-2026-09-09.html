4. Trends & Findings

Invite volume ramped up sharply through mid-year, then pulled back. Invites climbed from ~1,800–2,000/month in Q1 to a peak of 6,545 in June, then stepped back down to 5,069 (Jul) and 3,085 (Aug). September opens at just 3 invites in the first 8 days — worth confirming with the account whether this is a deliberate pause or a send-side issue, since it's a sharp drop from August's pace.

Reminders lag their originating invite month by ~30 days — and this explains the Jan/Feb/Mar pattern. Campaign 447's workflow sends the reminder step 720 hours (30 days) after the invite. Paired months line up closely: Jan invites (1,823) ≈ Feb reminders (1,807); Mar invites (2,083) ≈ Apr reminders (2,062); Jun invites (6,545) ≈ Jul reminders (6,542); Jul invites (5,069) ≈ Aug reminders (5,063). Feb showing 0 invites / 1,807 reminders and Mar showing 2,083 invites / 0 reminders is this same lag, not a data gap — each month is dominated by a different step of the same underlying send cohort.

Attribution share fell sharply as volume grew, and review rate fell with it. Jan–Mar ran 84–90% attributed with review rates of ~0.8–1.5%. From June onward, attribution dropped to 17–52% and review rate to 0.12–0.42%, even as invite volume was at its highest (Jun–Aug: 3,085–6,545/month). Organic reviews grew in absolute terms too (16 in Jun, 30 in Jul, 27 in Aug vs. 3–9/month in Q1), so this isn't purely an artifact of more invites diluting a fixed organic base.

Because attribution here uses Anyday's full invite history (not just the invite's own month), a genuinely late-converting customer would still show up as attributed whenever they eventually review — so the Jun–Aug dip is not simply "reviews haven't landed yet." It's worth flagging to the account: either recent invite cohorts are converting at a structurally lower rate than earlier in the year, or a growing share of Trustpilot reviews are coming from customers Anyday never invited. Both are actionable, but need the account team's input to disambiguate (e.g. wine season timing, order volume, deliverability changes).

Ratings have been stable and strong all year — every month sits between 4.37 and 4.86, with no month below 4.3. Negative reviews (≤3★) are consistently low (0–5/month, 22 total YTD out of 279, ~8%). Rating quality is not the story here; send volume, cadence, and attribution/conversion trends are.
