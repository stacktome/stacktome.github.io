Top Finding

First-time buyers are happy on arrival but disappointed at the 30-day mark. Service reviews written within 7 days of the order score the same for first-time and repeat buyers (4.73★ vs 4.79★, ~4% negative). Reviews written 31+ days after a first order fall to 4.14★ (service) and 3.82★ (product, 25% negative), while repeat buyers stay at ~4.5★. The gap is about whether the product worked, not about delivery or service. It should shape which customers are asked for a review, and when.

Key findings for campaign segmentation

  • 1
    A strong baseline. 84.8% positive, 4.43★ overall. Service reviews average 4.56★. Product reviews average 4.09★ and carry most of the negative risk (18.6% negative).
  • 2
    Purchase history is the strongest signal. Customers on their 6th+ order: 4.78★, 2–4% negative. First purchases: 4.45★ (service) / 3.81★ (product).
  • 3
    Timing matters as much as the segment. Reviews written 0–7 days after the order average 4.72–4.80★. After 30 days, efficacy complaints start ("didn't work", "made my skin worse").
  • 4
    Subscription friction appears in 24% of negative service reviews: unexpected auto-enrolment and difficulty cancelling.
  • 5
    The Trustpilot profile doesn't tell you the market. Most reviews on the "47 Skin USA" profile come from UK-store orders. Customers of the US store rate lower (4.25★ service), but it is a small sample.
  • 6
    Support contact is a warning sign only when it falls between order and review. Service reviews after such a ticket average 3.88★ (24.7% negative) vs 4.62★ with no ticket. Customers who contacted support earlier rate about the same as others, and resolved tickets are followed by healthy reviews (4.50★).

Orders are dispatched 0–1 days after purchase (scheduled dispatch date). Product reviews are almost all written 31–60 days after the order (925 of 993 order-matched product reviews; 4.18★). Reviews cluster at days 0–7 (service) and 31–60 (product), which suggests Trustpilot invitations may already be sent at those points. This should be confirmed in the Trustpilot account before new campaigns go live.

Why n=1,191 (not the ~2,011 order-linked service reviews): timing only uses reviews whose order is identified directly by the order number on the review (1,174 where the email also agrees, 17 on order number alone). The other 820 service reviews are linked only by email, and their order is inferred as the customer's latest order on or before the review date. That order may not be the one being reviewed, and by construction it is always dated before the review, so it would distort order-to-review time. Those 820 stay in the purchase-count and market analyses but not in the timing analysis. The timing-by-buyer table in section 4 uses the same rule and also leaves out 10 order-matched reviews with no identifiable purchase number.

9. Campaign Planning Insights
What the data suggests for later campaign setup — no campaigns created
  • 1
    Recent post-dispatch campaign (~5 days after dispatch). Dispatch happens 0–1 days after the order, so this lands around days 5–7, the highest-rated observed window (4–7 days: 4.72★, 4.7% negative). This is an observed pattern in organic reviews; it does not show that sending an invite at that time would produce the same rating. In that window first-time buyers rate like repeat buyers (4.73★ vs 4.79★), so they don't need to be excluded from a service/delivery-focused invite. Delivery complaints are 17% of negative service reviews and peaked in January, so consider holding invites for orders not yet delivered during peak season. Before launch, check whether Trustpilot's own invitations already go out at this point (review timing suggests they may) to avoid inviting customers twice.
  • 2
    Post-ticket campaign. Supported, with conditions. 10,222 customers contacted support in the period, and 8,849 of them have a closed ticket that is not about a subscription or a skin reaction (7,643 if refund and return contacts are also left out). Today only about 3% of customers leave a Trustpilot review within 30 days of a ticket closing (299 of 10,221), so there is room to grow. Support is a strength: 66.5% of positive service reviews from customers who contacted support praise the team. Reviews written after a resolved ticket look healthy (4.50★, 9.1% negative), while tickets still open at review time (4.41★, 13.5% negative) and tickets raised between the order and the review (service 3.88★, 24.7% negative) are the at-risk cases. Suggested rules: invite only once the ticket is closed, with a short delay after closure, and exclude subscription/cancellation, skin-reaction, and refund or return contacts. Keep the ask service-focused. Customers who have contacted support do not rate lower on products (first-time buyers 3.94★ vs 3.85★).
  • 3
    First-time buyer campaign / exclusions. First-purchase customers are the main source of negative reviews once efficacy is judged (31+ days: 4.14★ service, 3.82★ product, 25% negative), and trial-size serum buyers most of all. The evidence supports excluding first-time buyers from product- or efficacy-focused asks around day 30 and later, while still including them in the early post-dispatch invite. A first-time buyer campaign, if used, should be service-focused and early.
  • 4
    Loyal / repeat-customer campaign (Free Soul-style). Repeat customers are the strongest segment for product reviews: 2+ orders 4.53★ (8% negative), 6+ orders 4.78★ (2–4% negative). Subscribers perform about the same as other repeat buyers. This supports a repeat-purchase product-review campaign, for example triggered on the 2nd or 3rd+ order. Consider leaving out multi-step sets (3.81★ even among repeat buyers) and customers with recent subscription-cancellation contact.
