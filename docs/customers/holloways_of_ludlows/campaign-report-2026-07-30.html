Data note: the invite event log at this path is Delta-formatted (has a _delta_log), same as Lvanguard. Unlike Lvanguard, a plain-parquet read here does not silently miss recent data (both readers reach the same max date) — but it does return slightly more rows than the Delta read for at least one campaign (17,357 vs 17,235 for invite-tp), consistent with picking up superseded/compacted files the Delta log would exclude. This notebook always reads via format("delta") to avoid that risk.

Single account, three campaigns, one new since the last report. Campaigns were discovered dynamically (filesystem listing + GET /api/invites/v1/campaigns, cross-checked): invite-tp (461) and positive-closed-tickets (510) existed in the Jan–May report; invite-product (685) is new, first send 2026-06-12, and has a narrower schema (no _accountId/_campaignId/_projectId data columns) that required dropping those columns before unioning with the other two.

Correction (same day, 2026-07-30): the first draft's only review-attribution query filtered to event_subtype = 'review_order', so it never joined review_product events to invite history at all — every campaign's product-review count read as 0, which made invite-product (a product-review campaign) look like it had zero engagement. A parallel attribution query was added using the same order_id-first/email-fallback convention against product_rating instead of order_rating; Section 5 now shows the corrected per-campaign breakdown (invite-product: 9 attributed product reviews from 813 invites, all 5★).

5. Product Reviews

Holloways tracks product-level reviews (per SKU) separately from order-level reviews, via the same review event with event_subtype = 'review_product' and the product_rating column. This dimension doesn't exist in the Lvanguard reference report — it's carried over from Holloways' own schema because it materially changes the story: product quality is not where the problem is.

285 product reviews YTD, avg 4.89★ — only 5 rated ≤3★ (1.75%). This is far higher and far more stable than the order-level average (4.57★, with 41 of 387 ≤3★, 10.6%). The gap strongly suggests the products themselves are well-regarded and the order-level dissatisfaction is driven by fulfilment, delivery, and customer-service handling rather than the goods sold — consistent with the delivery-delay theme dominating the order-level negative reviews (Section 6).

6. Negative Review Themes

41 order reviews rated ≤3★ YTD, analysed by keyword match across the review text (English only — Holloways' customer base is UK/English, unlike Lvanguard's multi-market footprint). Categories overlap — a single review can describe both a delivery delay and unresponsive support.

📦
Delivery & Dispatch Delays
30 of 41 negative reviews (73%)

The dominant theme by a wide margin. Orders described as taking 8–12 weeks against a quoted 2–6 week window, repeatedly, across multiple independent reviews. Several name FedEx or third-party couriers specifically for missed/rescheduled delivery windows.

💬
Unresponsive Communication
22 of 41 negative reviews (54%)

Customers describe emailing multiple times with no reply, phone lines going unanswered, and having to chase repeatedly for basic order-status updates. Frequently compounds a delivery delay rather than standing alone.

📦
Wrong, Damaged, or Missing Items
11 of 41 negative reviews (27%)

Wrong size or item shipped, partial/incomplete multi-item orders, and faulty goods on arrival (one lamp arrived without a required ceiling plate; a replacement part was then the wrong colour/design).

💰
Refunds & Returns
15 of 41 negative reviews (37%)

Refunds eventually processed but slow, and at least one case of a legitimate return request being refused until the customer cited consumer legislation — echoes the same finding from the Jan–May report.

31 of 37 reviews rated ≤2★ (84%) have no matching support ticket (matched by email against hl_ticket). Given how consistently the negative review text describes customers repeatedly emailing and phoning without success, this gap plausibly reflects genuinely unresolved contact attempts rather than tickets simply logged elsewhere — worth a closer look given how specific and detailed the underlying complaints are.

7. Action Items

  • 🔴
    Delivery/dispatch delay is the dominant complaint driver — 73% of negative reviews. Repeated pattern of 8–12 week waits against 2–6 week quoted windows, often with no proactive update until the customer chases. This is the single biggest lever for rating improvement: either tighten the quoted lead times to match reality, or invest in proactive delay communication so customers aren't left guessing.
  • 🔴
    31 of 37 reviews rated ≤2★ (84%) have no matching support ticket. Given how many of these reviews describe the customer emailing and phoning repeatedly with no reply, confirm whether these contacts are being logged at all before assuming the gap is a matching artefact — the review text itself suggests a real response-handling problem, not just a data-matching one.
  • 🟡
    invite-product (685) shows early warning signs on engagement: 1.3% CTR, 1.9% unsub rate — both worse than the other two campaigns. Product-review attribution is not zero (corrected this report: 9 attributed reviews from 813 invites, 1.1%, all 5★ — see Section 5), but the weak CTR/unsub combination is still worth a look at targeting/messaging if it holds under more volume. Still under 7 weeks old.
  • 🟡
    June was a low point on both review rate (2.57%) and avg rating (3.73★), with a partial recovery in July (2.82%, 4.10★). Worth revisiting once more of July's reviews have landed to confirm whether this is a genuine recovery or still-incomplete data.
  • 🟢
    positive-closed-tickets (510) is Holloways' most efficient review-generation mechanism at 10.20% — ~4× the main invite-tp campaign, and every one of its attributed reviews is 3★+ (avg 4.88). Scaling this campaign to more resolved tickets would directly grow the attributed review count and lift the blended average rating.
  • 🟢
    Product quality is not the problem. Product-level reviews average 4.89★ with only 1.75% rated ≤3★, versus 10.6% at the order level. The gap between the two is the clearest evidence that fulfilment and service, not the goods themselves, are driving order-level dissatisfaction.

    Onboarding note: this is the first report from the new holloways_of_ludlow_data notebook (support_clone). 28.9% of order reviews (112 of 387) have neither order_id nor email and can never be attributed by either key — a genuine data gap on the review side worth raising with data engineering if campaign-level attribution completeness matters for future reporting.
