Review rate = all reviews ÷ invites sent × 100 (reminders excluded), shown at brand and group level. Attribution rate = reviews attributed to a review-invitation campaign ÷ all reviews received in the month × 100. A review is attributed when its order reference matches an order that any invitation campaign has emailed (full invitation history); the same method was used for September and October. These are different measures: the review rate is about volume against invites sent, the attribution rate is about how many of the reviews received can be traced back to a campaign.

Attribution declined for both brands. Carethy fell from 24.5% to 7.4% (12 to 4 attributed reviews) and Bulevip from 37.3% to 0.0% (22 to 0), so the combined rate fell 27.6 percentage points to 3.9%. Reviews with no matched invitation are now 96.1% of the group total (68.5% in September); this is a limit on what can be traced, not a count of reviews that were not prompted by an invitation.

Which campaigns contributed. In September Bulevip invite-tp-ES supplied 15 of the 34 attributed reviews (44%) and Carethy invite-tp-EU 7 (21%); the rest were spread across eight smaller campaigns. In October all 4 attributed reviews came from Carethy invite-tp-EU (960 invites, 3.50★ average); no Bulevip campaign and none of the other five Carethy campaigns had a review matched by the standard method. Attributed reviews per 1,000 invites fell from 9.7 to 0.8.

Confirmed. Invitation volume rose 36.5%, so the fall is not a drop in sending. Fewer reviews now arrive with an order reference: 20 of 103 (19.4%) in October against 51 of 108 (47.2%) in September (Carethy 25.9% from 42.9%, Bulevip 12.2% from 50.8%). Only 4 of the 20 October references match an emailed order by the standard method (34 of 51 in September). For Bulevip, none of its 49 October reviews carries a customer record or order number that links to an invitation, so no alternative field changes its 0.0% under the standard method.

Not confirmed. Why fewer reviews carry a usable reference. Carethy’s attributed share was 76–91% of reviews in January–May, stepped down in June and again in October (table below), which looks more like a change in how reviews are linked to orders than a change in customer behaviour, but the review data alone cannot establish that. Email click rate also fell, from 4.7% to 1.6%, which may be related. The October attribution rate is therefore best read as a floor on campaign influence, not a measure of it, and it would be worth confirming with the review integration that every invitation link passes the order reference for every campaign and market.

Consistency check. The same definition and full invitation history were applied in both months. Re-running September on current data returns 12 of 49 (Carethy), 22 of 59 (Bulevip) and 34 of 108 (group), identical to the published figures. Widening the invitation history through Oct 31 changes neither month, adding the closed-ticket invitation campaigns adds no October matches (one September Bulevip review), and every standard match in both months was written after an email to that order (4 of 4 in October, 34 of 34 in September).

Delivery & Carrier Failures
9 of 14 reviews (64%) · Carethy 7, Bulevip 2 · Sep 55%

Still the dominant theme. Carethy: an Australian order placed on Aug 13 and still not received on Oct 3 with tracking static since Aug 17; an order nine days old with no tracking; a parcel more than two weeks late; a courier whose help service kept returning an error; a UK parcel that took three failed delivery attempts and a collection trip; a Swiss customer who could not track the parcel and was billed at the border; and a Swedish order that took 12 days against 3–5 working days quoted. Bulevip: a parcel signed for by someone else, and a tracking number for an order that never arrived.

Damaged / Wrong / Missing Items
6 of 14 reviews (43%) · Carethy 3, Bulevip 3 · Sep 23%

Bulevip: four of five tahini jars arriving open or unsealed with oil spread over the packaging, a protein pack that burst in transit, and a jar that arrived open and partly spilled.

Two Carethy reviewers (1★, Oct 21 and Oct 25) say they could not reach customer support about a parcel that had not arrived after nine days and more than two weeks; one adds that an email failed to deliver. No refund-delay complaints appeared on Bulevip.

Import Duty / Customs Charges
1 of 14 reviews (7%) · Carethy 1 · Sep 14%

One Carethy reviewer in Switzerland was also asked to pay a border fee of 22 francs on a parcel they could not track. The German-language duty/VAT complaints seen in September did not recur.

All 11 reviews rated ≤2★ have no matching support ticket — Carethy 8 of 8, Bulevip 3 of 3; September (published): 12 of 18. Tickets are matched by email. 10 of the 11 carry no email at all (the review isn’t linked to an order), so "no ticket" mostly means "support status unknown", not "ignored by support". The one exception is an attributed Carethy 1★ (Oct 25, parcel not delivered after more than two weeks) that has an email and no ticket.

Action Items

  • 🔴
    Attribution fell to 3.9% (4 of 103) from 31.5% (34 of 108) on the standard method, and to 8.7% from 41.7% on a broader verified match; Bulevip had no standard-method matches. Only 19.4% of reviews carried an order reference (September 47.2%; Apr–May roughly 88–98%), and email click rate fell from 4.7% to 1.6%. Reviews with no match are not necessarily unprompted. Confirm that the review links in the Carethy and Bulevip invitation and reminder emails pass the order reference for every campaign and market, so reviews can be tied back to a campaign; until then the review rate and rating are the more reliable indicators.
  • 🔴
    Delivery complaints are 64% of ≤3★ reviews (9 of 14) vs 55% in September. Cases include an Australian order placed Aug 13 and still undelivered on Oct 3, orders with no usable tracking, a 12-day delivery to Sweden against 3–5 working days quoted, and a Bulevip parcel signed for by someone else. Review the carriers on the Australia, Sweden, Switzerland, UK and Spain routes and consider holding the invite until delivery is confirmed.
  • 🟡
    Contact the Oct 25 Carethy 1★ reviewer (parcel not delivered after more than two weeks, no way to reach support). This is the only negative review with an email and no ticket; a second Carethy reviewer (Oct 21) reports the same inability to reach support. Both need individual follow-up.
  • 🟡
    Opened or spilled jars drove 3 of Bulevip’s 5 reviews rated ≤3★ (tahini and other jars, Oct 11–31) and a smashed, under-packed order on Carethy. Check inner packaging for jars and powders and the seal checks before dispatch.
  • 🟡
    Click rate dropped on every campaign (group 1.6% from 4.7%) and unsubscribes rose to 191 from 134 (Carethy 2.45% of tracked sends, from 1.84%). Open rate held at 47.6%. Review the invitation and reminder content and links, starting with Carethy invite-tp-EU and invite-tp-DE (53 and 44 unsubscribes).
  • 🟡
    Invite sending was uneven within the month. Carethy sent 10, 2, 6, 3, 1 and 5 invites on Oct 15–16, 23–24 and 29–30, followed by 147, 191 and 182 on Oct 17, 25 and 31; Bulevip sent 3 invites on Oct 16, 17 and 22 and caught up the following days. Reminders ran every day. Confirm this batching is intended; it moves weekly review rates and delays invites for some customers.
  • 🟡
    Ticket matching is only possible for reviews with an email. 10 of 11 negative reviews have none, so the "no ticket" count (11) shows missing identity, not missing support follow-up. Linking reviews to orders would also lift the attribution rate.
  • 🟢
    Ratings improved: group 4.24★ to 4.45★, Carethy 4.08★ to 4.30★, Bulevip 4.37★ to 4.61★, with negatives at 10.7% (September 16.7%) and 77.7% of reviews at 5★. Review rate is 2.15% (September 3.08%): invites grew 36.5% while reviews slipped from 108 to 103.
