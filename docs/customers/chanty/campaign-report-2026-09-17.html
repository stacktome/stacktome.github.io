Yes — new Denmark/EU orders are currently being reached. Of the most recently shipped, already-matured orders (Sep 11–14), 10 of 11 (91%) have already received a request. That's the clearest read on whether the system is keeping up right now.

The lower 52% figure for the Aug 25–Sep 10 orders is almost entirely one specific, now-understood cause, not a spread-out problem. 22 of the 24 unreached orders in that window are older orders (placed weeks to months earlier) whose shipping information was only added or confirmed on Sep 8, all at once, in a single batch. That batch wasn't picked up correctly: 9+ days later, 20 of those 22 orders still haven't received a review request, compared to normally-shipped orders reaching customers within days. We've flagged this specific batch so it can be corrected, and to prevent it recurring for future batches.

US — orders shipped since launch (Sep 15 – today)

Too early to measure yet. The US campaign only started sending Sep 15, two days before this report, and (as in Section 2) a shipped order needs a few days to become eligible for a request. Only 1 order has shipped since launch, and it isn't old enough yet to be due — so there isn't a meaningful sample of new post-launch orders to check. The 51 requests sent on day one were a one-time catch-up covering existing older orders that had shipped before the campaign was turned on, not new post-launch volume.

Recommend re-checking this specific question in the next report, once enough new US orders have shipped and had time to mature.

Separately, a data-quality gap affects both stores going forward: looking at the last 60 days overall, 25 Denmark/EU orders (15%) and 7 US orders (21%) are missing the shipment-date information the system needs to know when a customer becomes eligible for a request at all. These orders won't receive a request under the current setup, regardless of how much time passes, unless that data gap is fixed at the source — see Section 7.

7. Issues Found

  • 🔴
    A batch of older orders had their shipping information added later, and wasn't picked up correctly. We traced 22 Denmark/EU orders, placed weeks to months earlier, whose shipping details were only added on Sep 8. 9+ days later, 20 of those 22 still haven't received a review request, versus normally-shipped orders reaching customers within days. See Section 6 for detail — this is the most concrete, fixable issue found in this review.
  • 🟡
    15–21% of recent orders on both stores are missing shipping-date information and can never receive a review request as a result, no matter how much time passes. Worth checking with whoever manages order data on both storefronts.
  • 🟡
    Any customer who has ever filed a support ticket is permanently excluded from review requests, even if the issue was minor and fully resolved. This is a deliberate design choice, but it steadily shrinks the reachable customer base over time — worth revisiting if increasing review volume is a priority.
  • 🟡
    The US Support Follow-up Campaign has never been turned on. Turning it on would add another source of review requests for the US store.
  • 🔵
    Denmark/EU's Support Follow-up Campaign hasn't sent anything since Aug 25 — but we checked, and this is working as intended, not a bug. Its list only ever had 2 qualifying customers (both a positively-rated, closed support case), both were correctly requested and later cycled off the list once served. There simply haven't been any new customers matching that criteria on the Denmark/EU side since Aug 5. No action needed; it will resume automatically the next time a support case closes with a positive rating.
  • 🔵
    A small number of sends can't currently be confirmed: 8 of 146 Denmark/EU sends (5.5%) and 1 of 51 US sends (2%) — see Section 3 for detail. This doesn't affect the delivery rates reported here, which are based only on confirmed sends. We're monitoring and will follow up if it continues.

8. Why Review Volume Is Currently Low

Both programs are genuinely new — Denmark/EU started less than 4 weeks ago, the US store only 2 days before this report. Reviews typically take days to weeks to arrive after a request goes out, so a small count so far is expected on timing alone.

The recipient lists feeding both Main Review Campaigns are currently small (39 qualifying customers in Denmark/EU, 4 in the US) — requests can only go to whoever is on the list at a given time, and the list refills gradually as new orders age past the shipping-plus-a-few-days mark.

On top of that, the issues in Section 7 are actively suppressing volume: the shipping-date backfill issue that's currently blocking 20 known Denmark/EU orders, a meaningful share of orders that can never be reached due to missing shipping data, and a permanent exclusion rule that grows over time. Fixing those would meaningfully increase how many customers are asked for a review, independent of how long the programs have been running. (Denmark/EU's Support Follow-up Campaign, by contrast, is not a factor here — it's simply waiting on a support-side event that hasn't recurred yet.)

9. Recommended Next Steps

  • 1.
    Fix the Sep 8 batch issue and send review requests to the 20 known-affected Denmark/EU orders, and check that future batches of older orders don't hit the same gap.
  • 2.

  • 3.
    Decide whether the "any past support ticket, forever" exclusion rule should be softened (e.g. only exclude recent or unresolved tickets) to keep more customers eligible over time.
  • 4.
    Consider switching on the US Support Follow-up Campaign to add volume.
  • 5.
    Re-check this report's numbers again in 2–4 weeks once both programs have more history — particularly the US store, which is only 2 days into sending. No action needed on the Denmark/EU Support Follow-up Campaign itself — just monitor.
