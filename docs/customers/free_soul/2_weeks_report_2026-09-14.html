What Actually Drove the Decline — Contribution by Source

The account-wide average fell from 4.300★ (258 total rating-points / 60 reviews) to 3.937★ (311 / 79), a change of −0.363 pts. Each source's exact contribution to that change is (source's current-period rating-points ÷ 79) − (source's prior-period rating-points ÷ 60) — these six numbers sum exactly to −0.363, so this is not an approximation.

The single largest negative contributor is the "no order ID" bucket (−0.384 pts), narrowly ahead of invite-skus (−0.378 pts) — but that bucket's campaign source is unknown, not proven non-campaign. These 15/13 reviews (prior/current) carry no order_id at all, so they can't be checked against invite history by any key; unlike the separate "Organic (has order ID)" row above — which genuinely was checked against full invite history and confirmed not to match — the no-order-ID group could in principle include customers invited by any of the four campaigns, invite-skus included. Combined, the two non-campaign-attributable rows (confirmed-organic + unknown-source) pulled the average down by −0.491 pts — more than the entire net decline. Meanwhile the four named campaigns together contributed +0.129 pts (0.146 + (−0.002) + (−0.378) + 0.363) to the change in the overall, all-review average — not because their own average improved (it didn't: the 4-campaign combined average itself fell from 4.50★ to 4.05★, per the Review Rate table above), but because +0.129 pts is a contribution figure computed against the full account-wide denominator (60 then 79 total reviews), and reflects the campaigns' growing share of that total pool as much as their own rating movement. In plain terms: campaign-attributed reviews got both more numerous and somewhat lower-rated, but the volume growth was large enough that their net pull on the account-wide average was still positive, even though, taken on their own terms, they declined too (just less than invite-skus alone, and much less than the account-wide figure). This does not mean invite-skus is blameless (see Section 8) — only that its role in the total account-wide number can't be cleanly isolated from the unknown-source bucket.

Data Validation & Caveats

Freshness
Reviews: min/max ts_event exactly 2026-08-17 / 2026-09-13 — full window covered, generation day (Sep 14) correctly excluded.
Invites: min/max _date exactly 2026-08-17 / 2026-09-13 — same.
Deduplication
Invites: 37,872 raw rows in-window = 37,872 distinct (campaign, order, send-type, date) — no duplicate-row artifact (unlike the pattern seen on other accounts' parquet reads; all reads here used Delta format per the account's known parquet-overcount fix).
Reviews: 139 raw rows = 139 distinct order_review_id.
Settling / attribution lag risk
The current window ends only 1 day before generation vs. 15 days for the prior window — late-window invitees (sent Sep 10–13) have had little time to review, so current-period review counts and review rates are provisional and will likely rise on the next report, understating true conversion for now.
Checked for rating bias from this: among current-period same-window attributed reviews, average invite-to-review lag is 3.51 days for 4–5★ reviewers (n=35) vs 3.86 days for ≤3★ reviewers (n=14) — no meaningful gap, so the rating comparison itself doesn't show an obvious "angry customers respond faster" skew in this window, even though total volume is still settling.
Attribution methodology
Same convention as prior reports: join review order_id to all-time invite history (not just in-window invites), tie-break ties toward the lowest campaign ID, classify no-match-with-order-id as "organic," no-order-id-at-all separately. ~34% of current-period reviews (27 of 79) carry no order ID — consistent with the Sep-1 report's flagged data-completeness gap for this account, unresolved as of this analysis.
Multi-campaign overlap check (tie-break validation)
Checked whether the lowest-campaign-ID tie-break could be materially undercounting invite-skus: 2 of 42 prior attributed reviews and 4 of 64 current attributed reviews (5–6%) matched invite history from more than one campaign — in every case, the overlap was invite-tp (237) & invite-skus (310) together (no overlaps found involving invite-closed or invite-loyal), and all were tie-broken to invite-tp (237) as the lower ID.
Directional effect: the 6 reallocated reviews skew positive (ratings 5,5,5,4 current; 1,5 prior) — if credited to invite-skus instead, its recomputed average would move from 4.71★→3.80★ to a less severe 4.50★→4.00★, and its negative rate from 7.1%→33.3% to 12.5%→26.3%. So the tie-break convention is not understating invite-skus's decline — if anything the officially reported numbers are slightly more severe than an invite-skus-inclusive alternative would show. Given the small sample (≤27% of invite-skus's own count either period) and that correcting it would soften rather than strengthen the case against invite-skus, no change was made to the primary tables — this reflects the account's existing, consistently-applied attribution convention, not an error requiring correction.

Conclusion — Was invite-skus the Primary Driver?

invite-skus is clearly the strongest identifiable campaign-side driver of the rating decline — but we cannot conclusively say it was the primary driver of the total account-wide decline.

What the numbers support:

1. invite-skus deteriorated more sharply than any other campaign: its own average fell from 4.71★ to 3.80★ and its negative rate rose from 7.1% to 33.3% — both the largest moves of any of the four campaigns, and its current negatives are now dominated by subscription/cancellation complaints rather than the taste complaints the August segment fix addressed (that fix remains intact and is not the cause).

2. Its contribution to the account-wide change (−0.378 pts) is roughly equal to the entire account decline (−0.363 pts) — among identifiable, campaign-attributed sources, nothing else comes close to explaining the decline on its own.

3. However, the "no order ID" bucket also contributed −0.384 pts, and it cannot be assigned to any campaign — not because it's confirmed non-campaign (unlike the separate "Organic, has order ID" row, which was checked against full invite history and genuinely doesn't match), but because there's no order_id to check at all. Its true source, including how much of it may in fact be invite-skus traffic, is unknown.

4. Because that unknown-source bucket is at least as large a negative contributor as invite-skus, we cannot conclusively say invite-skus was the primary driver of the total account decline — only that it is the strongest driver we can actually identify and attribute. A materially different split between "true invite-skus" and "true no-order-id campaign source" within that unknown bucket could shift the balance either way.

Bottom line: invite-skus is the one campaign worth fixing based on this window's data regardless — its own trend is real, large, and specific (subscription/cancellation friction, not taste), and no plausible reallocation of the unknown-source bucket makes that go away. But "primarily responsible for the account-wide decline" is a stronger claim than the data can support, given a comparably-sized pool of reviews whose campaign origin we simply don't know.
