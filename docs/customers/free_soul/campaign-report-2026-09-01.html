August invite volume rose 13.4% over July (20,842 vs 18,384), driven almost entirely by invite-skus (310) more than doubling (1,577→3,457) after a segment-eligibility fix landed (see Segment Setup Analysis). Reviews received in August (147, all sources) are essentially flat vs July (158) and the average rating held steady (4.38★→4.43★), with negatives down both in count (28→23) and share (17.7%→15.6%).

Methodology note, carried forward from the Jun–Jul report's June/July treatment: July is fully settled (over a month closed at generation time) and its 84-review, 0.46% figure is attributed-only — matched to a specific invite by order ID. August closed only one day before this report was generated, so its attribution is still catching up: some late-August invitees haven't had time to review yet, and some will still convert in September. Per the Jun–Jul report's own convention, August's headline figures (147 reviews, 4.43★, 23 negatives) are all reviews received (attributed + organic), not attributed-only, and should not be read as directly comparable to July's attributed-only rate. August's attributed count already stands at 102, ahead of July's fully-settled 84 — for comparison, June's fully-settled attributed count (once corrected in the Jun–Jul report) was only 30, well below July's 84, so August continuing to run ahead of July is a genuine continuation of that recovery, not just a lag artifact. The exact August figure will still move as trailing conversions arrive; treat it as a strong early read, not a final one.

What each bucket means

"Same-window" = invite and review fall in the same calendar month (the cleanest read of that month's own conversion). "Carryover" = review matches an invite sent in an earlier month — a real, attributed conversion, just not from that month's own sends. "Organic" = the review has an order ID but it matches no invite in history — genuinely un-invited. "No order ID" = roughly a third of all reviews both months (38% Jul, 27% Aug) carry no order ID whatsoever and cannot be checked against invite history by any key — a data-completeness gap on the review event itself, not an attribution-logic failure. This is a materially larger unattributable share than most other accounts and worth a closer look at whether Trustpilot review submissions are reliably passing order references back to Free Soul's tracking.
