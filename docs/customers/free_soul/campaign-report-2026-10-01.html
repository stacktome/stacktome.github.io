Method. The attribution methodology is unchanged from the August report. The headline Review Rate, however, now follows the current reporting standard (below) and differs from the August report's attributed-only headline. The invite log is Delta-formatted and was read as Delta (never plain parquet); campaign IDs were checked against the invite path (237, 240, 310, 424, plus the response campaign 742 and test campaigns, which are excluded). Review Rate (Total) = all reviews received ÷ invites sent × 100 (the current reporting standard). Attributed-only rates (reviews matched to a specific invite by order ID) remain secondary figures. Negatives are reviews rated ≤3★. August figures are the values published in the August report, including its 4.43★ average and 23 negatives; a fresh recount of August from today's data gives 4.45★ and 22 (one August review's rating has since changed) and otherwise matches, including every per-campaign figure. The August report's headline Review Rate was attributed-only (0.49%); on the all-reviews basis used here, August is 0.71% (147 ÷ 20,842) and July is 0.86% (158 ÷ 18,384).

Sending stopped on Sep 25 and the average rating fell. No invite or reminder was sent on any campaign from Sep 26 to Sep 30 (sending resumed on Oct 1), and invite-skus (310) stopped earlier, after Sep 14, and is now shown as inactive in the app. Total September volume (40,267) is almost exactly August's (40,538) and July's (40,603): sends have run at about 40,500 a month since July, against 52,800–60,700 a month from January to June, and in both August and September the month's last send was on the 26th or 25th. That pattern looks like a monthly send limit; it should be confirmed with the account team (see Action Items).

The group average rating fell from 4.43★ to 4.06★ and negatives rose from 15.6% to 24.6% of reviews (35 of 142). Every bucket moved down: campaign-attributed reviews (4.15★, from 4.45★), organic reviews with an order ID (2.33★) and reviews with no order ID (4.08★). Taste is still the largest complaint (20 of 35), subscription and cancellation complaints grew in count (11), and delivery and customer-service complaints fell back.

Invites rose +2.0% on August (20,842→21,258), with reminders −3.5% (19,696→19,009). Reviews were roughly flat (147→142), so Review Rate (Total) eased from 0.71% to 0.67%. Campaign-attributed reviews were also flat (102→100). The invite-skus (310) turnaround reported in August did not hold: its average fell from 4.85★ to 4.05★ and 5 of its 19 attributed reviews were negative, four of them about subscriptions, even though the segment still enforces the two-purchase minimum.

Attribution for September is provisional: customers invited in the last days before the Sep 25 stop have had little time to review, and some conversions will still arrive in October. Same-window reviews (invite and review in the same month) were 84 of 142; August had 89 of 147.

Action Items

  • 1
    Confirm why sending stops before month-end, and whether a monthly send limit applies. No invites or reminders were sent on any campaign from Sep 26 to Sep 30 (August: from Aug 27), and monthly volume has sat at about 40,300–40,600 since July against 52,800–60,700 in January–June. If a monthly cap is the cause, the last days of each month are being skipped: pacing the sends across the full month (or lifting the cap) would reach those customers while their order is fresh, and would make each month's review rate directly comparable.
  • 2
    Confirm whether invite-skus (310) was deliberately switched off. It is inactive in the app and last sent on Sep 14, the day of the rating-decline analysis, which named it the strongest campaign-side driver of the fall. The August “turnaround” (4.85★, 1 negative) did not hold in September (4.05★, 5 negatives of 19). Four of those 5 are about subscriptions, despite the two-purchase minimum. Decide whether to restart it, and if so address subscription friction first.
  • 3
    Review the subscription and cancellation journey. 11 of 35 negatives (31%) concern it, in the Sep reviews' own words “hidden”, “impossible to cancel” and an unexpected automatic reorder after a first purchase. This is a fixable, non-product driver that appears in invite-skus, invite-loyal and organic reviews alike.
  • 4
    Taste remains the largest complaint (20 of 35, 57%), but the average fell in every group. The group average dropped 0.37★ to 4.06★ and negatives rose to 24.6%. Delivery and customer-service complaints each roughly halved in share, so the extra negatives are mostly taste and subscription. Worth checking whether product or formulation changes, or new traffic sources (TikTok Shop, Instagram ads), coincide with the fall.
  • 5
    Check why the closed-tickets segment (554) is now empty. invite-closed volume fell to 52 invites (77 in August) and the segment has 0 eligible customers. Confirm whether tickets are still being closed and tagged as the segment expects, or whether the segment logic needs attention. The question raised in the Mar–Jun report remains open.
  • 6
    Keep the order-ID gap on the list. 36 of 142 reviews (25%; August 27%) carry no order ID and cannot be tied to a campaign, and 13 of the 35 negatives are organic. Worth confirming that Trustpilot submissions (especially from marketplace orders) pass an order reference back for this account.
  • 7
    Template A/B: still no consistent winner. TP rated higher in September for invite-tp and invite-loyal, branded converted slightly better overall. With cells of 4–27 reviews, continue another month before choosing. The “recovery” segment (3 customers) is explained as the feed for the response flow (campaign 742) and needs no action beyond keeping it out of invite counts.
