Definitions. Review Rate (Total) = all reviews received ÷ invites sent × 100 (reminders excluded). Attribution Rate = reviews attributed to a campaign ÷ all reviews received × 100. A review is attributed only when the same order it reviews received a review invitation that was sent before the review was written; a customer invited about a different order is not counted. These are separate measures: Review Rate is about volume against invites sent, Attribution Rate is about how many reviews can be traced to an invitation. Reviews that cannot be traced are shown as “unmatched” or “no order ID”, not as organic: they may include customers who were invited. Negatives are reviews rated ≤3★. Each review is counted once. The same rules were applied to September, and re-running September reproduces its published review, attribution and per-campaign figures, so no September restatement was needed. The response flow (campaign 742) and test campaigns are not review invitations and are excluded from every invite, send and rate figure.

More reviews at a slightly better rating. Reviews rose 28.9% to 183 (142 in September) on 2.3% fewer invites, so Review Rate (Total) rose to 0.88% from 0.67%. The average rating recovered to 4.17★ from 4.06★ and the negative share eased to 22.4% from 24.6%. invite-tp produced the most attributed reviews (63, avg 4.19★), and invite-loyal had the best average among the large campaigns (4.63★, 3 of 32 negative).

Subscription and cancellation complaints grew, and invite-skus (310) rated lower. Subscription and cancellation now appear in 19 of 41 negatives (46%), up from 11 of 35 (31%), taking over from taste (15 of 41, 37%, down from 57%) as the main theme. Delivery (14, 34%) and customer-service complaints (11, 27%) also rose in share. invite-skus resumed sending on Oct 6 (4,191 invites) and its 25 attributed reviews averaged 3.60★ with 9 negative, against 4.05★ and 5 negative in September.

Invites fell 2.3% on September (21,258→20,768) and reminders rose 2.1% (19,009→19,407), so total sends were almost unchanged (40,267→40,175). Attributed reviews rose from 100 to 124, but unmatched reviews rose faster (42→59), so the Attribution Rate slipped 2.6 points to 67.8% (70.4%). The share of reviews with no order ID was 26.8% (49 of 183; September 25.4%); these reviews cannot be matched to a campaign and are not necessarily unprompted. Same-window reviews (invite and review in the same month) were 109 of 183; September had 84 of 142.

Template Performance: Branded vs TP

The template mix changed in October. invite-tp splits its invites about evenly between the branded template (invite-brand) and the TP template (invite-tp); invite-closed stays TP-only; invite-skus now sends only the TP template (4,191 invites; September about half and half); and invite-loyal moved mostly to TP (8,671 of 9,918 invites; September about half and half). Reviews are matched to the template of the invitation sent to the same order, with the invitation sent before the review was written; a review is placed in the month it was written. September is restated on this basis. The September report grouped reviews by the month the invitation was sent (44 branded and 38 TP across invite-tp, invite-skus and invite-loyal); those figures reproduce exactly from the same data. Action Items

  • 1
    Fix the subscription and cancellation experience first (19 of 41 negatives, 46%, from 31%). Customers say they received no reminder before a renewal, could not find or use a working cancel option, were charged after cancelling, or had an address change that did not carry over to repeat orders. Sending a clear reminder before each renewal, making cancellation a single visible step and applying account address changes to subscriptions would address most of these cases directly.
  • 2
    Review invite-skus (310) now that it is sending again. It resumed on Oct 6 with 4,191 invites, and its attributed rating fell to 3.60★ (9 of 25 negative) from 4.05★, with subscription and cancellation the most common cause. Address subscription friction for this audience before it scales further.
  • 3
    Follow up individually on open cases. Examples in October reviews: a parcel delivered to the wrong door and an order that never arrived after an address failed to save (invite-skus), a double charge with no reply (invite-tp), refund requests said to be ignored, and a repeat subscription order sent to an old address. Proactive, visible resolution of these cases would lower the delivery and customer-service share, which rose to 34% and 27% of negatives.
  • 4
    Keep invitation timing consistent and delivery-based. Consider inviting every customer at the same interval after their order is delivered. The daily sending pattern in October varied: invites were near zero on Oct 16–19, reminders were zero on Oct 6–7, and no review emails were recorded on Oct 28–31. The data does not show why sending paused on those days or whether any customers were affected, so it is worth confirming whether these pauses were intended and reviewing how sends are paced across the month.
  • 5
    Taste is still the second-largest complaint (15 of 41, 37%) but its share fell from 57%. Several reviewers describe gritty texture and artificial sweetness in specific flavours; keep passing this to the product team, and consider clearer flavour and mixing guidance on the product page.
  • 6
    Keep the order-ID gap on the list. 49 of 183 reviews (26.8%; September 25.4%) carry no order ID and cannot be tied to a campaign, and a further 10 carry an order ID with no earlier invitation. Worth confirming that Trustpilot submissions (especially from marketplace orders) pass an order reference back for this account, which would lift the Attribution Rate.
  • 7
    Template A/B: still no consistent winner. Branded led on review rate within invite-tp in October, TP led on rating elsewhere in September, and the template mix changed in invite-skus and invite-loyal, so results are not like-for-like. Keep the test running where both templates remain live before choosing.
