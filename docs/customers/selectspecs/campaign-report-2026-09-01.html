Period Comparison

Methodology

Jul 1–31 and Aug 1–31 are both full, fully-closed calendar months — the first true month-over-month comparison for this account (prior reports compared partial/rolling windows: Jun4–17, Jun18–Jul1, and Jun1–17-vs-Jul1–17). This report also introduces real review-to-invite attribution for the first time on this account, using the same method as elsewhere: each review is matched against invite history via order_id first, falling back to email when order_id is absent, with no date restriction on the invite side of the join — an invite from any earlier month can still attribute a review received this month. Reviews split into four buckets: attributed (same-window) — matched to an invite sent in the same month as the review, the closest thing to a verified conversion; attributed (carryover) — matched to an invite from an earlier month; organic — has an order_id or email but matches no invite in history; and no-join-key — has neither field, so it cannot be checked against invite history at all (a real, non-trivial share of this account's reviews — see Attribution Split below, not an empty edge case). Review Rate (Total) = all reviews received that month ÷ invites sent that month — a raw ratio, not a verified conversion figure. Review Rate (Attributed, same-window) = same-window-attributed reviews only ÷ invites sent — the closer approximation to a true conversion rate, shown as a secondary reference figure throughout this report. A further, more conservative raw ratio (reviews ÷ all distinct emailed orders, invite or reminder) is also shown for continuity with prior reports.

Reading the volume drop honestly

Invite volume fell 21.6% (7,448→5,842 distinct orders) — but this is not a broad-based decline. The core invite-EN (210) campaign, which carries the overwhelming majority of volume, dropped only 3.6% month-over-month (5,973→5,757 distinct orders in July vs August, per the campaign breakdown below) — well within normal variation. Nearly the entire drop traces to a single campaign going silent: positive-reviews (622) sent 1,369 distinct-order invites in July and zero in August — its last send was Jul 22, 2026, confirmed via a second data source. See Recommended Actions. Unlike the June programme-launch backlog blasts documented in earlier reports, there is no unusual day-level invite spike at either month's boundary this time (daily sends Jul 27–Aug 5 ranged 4–500, consistent with ordinary day-to-day variation, not a batch artifact) — the rate comparison here is not being flattered by a denominator quirk the way the Jun-vs-Jul comparison was.

Theme shift, month over month

Lens/prescription quality overtook delivery as the single largest negative theme in August (41% vs 28% in July), while delivery/dispatch held roughly flat (38% vs 37%) rather than declining. Customer service and frame-quality shares were both broadly stable. This is a directional shift worth watching for a second month before treating it as a trend rather than sample noise (n=29 in August).

Escalation-worthy

Several August 1★ reviews describe open, unresolved cases at the time of writing — an order never received with an ignored refund request and no response to repeated emails and calls (Aug 29), and one parcel tracked as misrouted to Mexico instead of Malta (Aug 25). As with prior reports, this pattern — invites reaching customers whose problem is still live — turns unresolved private issues into public 1★ reviews.

Key Findings

Quality held up despite a real volume drop

Every top-line quality metric improved in August even though invite volume fell 21.6%: the raw review-to-invite ratio (Review Rate Total) rose 4.23%→5.60% (+32%), and the more conservative same-window attributed rate rose even more sharply, 2.32%→3.41% (+47%) — see Attribution Split. Reviews collected still grew slightly (315→327), average rating improved (4.33★→4.48★), and 1-star share fell (10.2%→7.0%). None of this improvement is a denominator artifact from a backlog blast — daily invite sends around both month boundaries look ordinary, unlike the June programme-launch spikes flagged in earlier reports.

The volume drop is a single-campaign story, not a programme-wide one

positive-reviews (622) sent 1,369 invites in July and none in August (last send: Jul 22) — confirmed via a second data source, which returned no activity for it in the August window. This one campaign accounts for 85% of the entire month's invite-volume decline; the core invite-EN campaign fell only 3.6%. Whether this was an intentional pause or an unnoticed stoppage should be confirmed with the account/marketing-ops team.

Lens/prescription quality is now the leading complaint

12 of August's 29 negative reviews (41%) describe wrong, blurred, or otherwise unusable lenses — up from 28% in July and now ahead of delivery delay (38%) as the top theme. Several describe the customer needing to visit an independent optician to get a usable pair. Worth flagging to SelectSpecs as a possible glazing/QC signal distinct from the already-known delivery problem.

Recommended Actions

  • 1
    Confirm whether the positive-reviews (622) campaign stoppage on Jul 22 was intentional. It drove 1,369 invites in July, served a distinct purpose (post-resolution follow-up) from the core programme, and now sends nothing. Re-enabling it, if the stoppage was unintended, is the single largest lever available to grow review volume without touching the core programme.
  • 2
    Escalate the lens/prescription quality signal to SelectSpecs. Now the leading complaint theme (41% of negatives, up from 28% in July), with multiple reviews describing customers needing an independent optician to get a wearable pair. Watch whether this holds as a trend or reverts next month.
  • 3
    Delivery/logistics failures remain a steady ~38% of negatives, including at least one parcel misrouted to a different country entirely (Mexico instead of Malta). Combined with the open, unresolved cases visible in this month's reviews, suppressing invites for orders with an undelivered or open-refund status (recommended in the July report) remains unactioned and still relevant.
  • 4
    Address the no-join-key gap on ~28% of reviews. These reviews carry neither an order reference nor an email and can never be matched to an invite, capping the attributed share of every future report at roughly 72% no matter how good the attribution logic is. Worth raising with data engineering as a review-event completeness issue. Separately, future reports could also cross-check negative reviews against support-ticket records, the same check already used on other accounts, if a suitable data source becomes available for this account.
  • 5
    Don't treat 5–6% as a confirmed steady-state review rate yet. That band came from the prior report's bi-weekly windows (Jun18–Jul1: 5.33%, Jul1–17: 6.26%) using a simpler raw-ratio method with no attribution check. This report's first two full-calendar-month readings tell a more mixed story: Review Rate (Total) was 4.23% (Jul) and 5.60% (Aug); the more conservative same-window-attributed rate was 2.32% (Jul) and 3.41% (Aug). Window shape and methodology both move this number too much to call a baseline on two data points — treat it as trending up, not settled, and revisit after a third full month.
