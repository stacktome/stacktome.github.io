Headline Finding — a Tracking Gap, Not Just a Rate Change

Most reviews in the July window can't be matched to anything

Before comparing rates, the join had to be checked properly: does the order_id on each review actually match an invite ever sent to that order? Doing this exposed that 77.5% of Jul 22–31 reviews (62 of 80) carry no order_id and no email at all — they cannot be matched to an invitation by any method, so their true origin (invited vs. organic) is genuinely unknown, not just "organic." In the Aug 1–10 window this "no join key" share drops to 14.4% (15 of 104). The daily trend shows a sharp change right at the month boundary:

This looks like a data-pipeline fix that landed right around Aug 1–2, not a gradual trend — June overall was 36.2% missing, July 28.3%, August (so far) 14.4%, but the days immediately before Aug 1 were far worse (70–100%) than the monthly average suggests. This means the Jul 22–31 vs Aug 1–10 comparison is not apples-to-apples on raw review counts — a large part of any rate change reflects newly-visible attribution, not necessarily a change in customer behaviour. See below for what this implies about the Jul 22 report's own headline numbers.

Period Comparison

Methodology

Jul 22–31 and Aug 1–10 are the two requested 10-day windows. Unlike the naive "reviews in period ÷ invites in period" ratio used in earlier reports, this analysis performs a real join: every review's order_id is matched against the full invite history (all-time, not just the window) via order_review_id-keyed attribution, distinguishing (a) invite-attributed, same-window — the order was invited within this same 10-day window, (b) invite-attributed, carryover — the order was invited at some earlier date, this review just landed in the window, (c) confirmed organic — the order has an order_id but was never invited, and (d) no join key — no order_id or email on the review, so attribution is unknowable. Verified invitation conversion rate = invite-attributed same-window reviews ÷ distinct orders invited in that same window (email_send_type = 'invite'). Alongside it, "Total review rate" = all reviews landing in the window (attributed + organic + no-join-key combined) ÷ invites sent in that window — this is not attribution-dependent, so the order_id tracking gap doesn't affect it; it answers "how much total review volume came in per invite sent," regardless of whether any individual review can be proven to trace back to an invite. For a period-over-period read of non-invited volume, "Organic reviews — total" combines (c) and (d) — no-join-key reviews are grouped with confirmed organic as a best-estimate, since every no-join-key review in both windows is a Trustpilot-sourced row with no invite trace, matching the profile of the confirmed-organic rows rather than the invite-attributed ones; this is a working assumption, not a certainty, given (d)'s attribution is technically unknowable.

Gap-adjusted estimate for July

Of Jul 22–31's 18 reviews that do carry an order_id, 6 (33.3%) were same-window attributed, 4 (22.2%) carryover, 8 (44.4%) confirmed organic. Applying that same split to the 62 unkeyed reviews as a rough estimate (assuming the tracking gap is random with respect to invitation status, which cannot be verified) yields an estimated ~21 additional same-window-attributed reviews, for an adjusted rate of roughly ~1.47% (26.7/1,820) — still well below August's as-observed 3.94%. Even under this generous adjustment, August looks structurally better, but the true size of the gap is uncertain and this estimate should not be quoted as a hard number.

Data Freshness & Prior-Report Caveat

The Aug 1–10 window is not closed

Generated today, Aug 10, 2026. The review table's MAX(ts_event) is Aug 9 — no Aug 10 reviews have landed yet — and invites sent late in the August window (Aug 5–10) have had only 0–5 days to convert vs 10–20 days for the July cohort. Expect August's rate to climb further as the window matures.

This calls the Jul 22 report's headline rate into question

The Jul 22 report (Jun 1–17 vs Jul 1–17) computed "review rate" as reviews landing in period ÷ invites sent in period — a same-window count, without verifying via order_id that those reviews actually came from invited orders. Given what this analysis found — most reviews in that era carry no order_id, and the ones that do split roughly 40/30/30 between same-window / carryover / organic — the previously published 6.26% (Jul 1–17) and 3.05% (Jun 1–17) figures are very likely overstated as measures of true invitation-to-review conversion; they mostly reflect coincidental timing between two independently-growing counts, inflated further by the same order_id gap documented here. This report's numbers are not directly comparable to those earlier ones as a result. Recommend re-running the Jun–Jul comparison with proper order_id-based attribution before citing those percentages again.

Key Findings

Total review rate is up 50% and isn't affected by the tracking gap

Because it doesn't require order_id attribution, total review rate (4.40% → 6.60%) is the one number in this report unaffected by the pipeline issue above — it's a straight count of all reviews landing in the window divided by invites sent, the same formula used in the Jul 22 report's benchmarks. Aug 1–10's 6.60% sits right in the 6%+ steady-state band that report identified, and is comparable to it in a way the attribution-based rates in this report are not.

August looks better even after accounting for the tracking gap

The as-observed jump (0.33% → 3.94%) is not a reliable read of behavioural change — most of it is the order_id gap closing. But even the conservative gap-adjusted estimate for July (~1.47%) sits well below August's as-observed 3.94%, so the direction of improvement is likely real; only the magnitude is uncertain.

Carryover matters — invites don't convert same-window

In August, carryover reviews (invited before the window, reviewed within it) are 21.2% of all reviews — nearly as large as the same-window-attributed share was for the entire July window. This confirms the account's known pattern of multi-week review lag and is why same-window-only rates will always understate a campaign's eventual yield.

Organic volume swung hard the other way

Using the consistent, combined definition (confirmed organic + no-join-key) for both periods, organic reviews fell from 70 (87.5% of all reviews) in Jul 22–31 to 20 (19.2%) in Aug 1–10 — a swing of the same size as the invite-attributed increase, and largely the mirror image of the tracking-gap finding above rather than a separate signal. Read the mix shift (organic-dominated → invite-dominated), not the organic count in isolation, since a chunk of what's now counted as "invite-attributed" in August would very likely have been counted the same way in July had the order_id been captured.

Rating quality improved

Average rating across all reviews (regardless of attribution) rose from 4.36★ to 4.46★. Sample sizes are small (80–104 reviews per window), so treat this as directional.

  • 2

  • 3
    Re-measure this comparison once the order_id fix has had 30+ days to mature and both windows are fully closed. Today's numbers mix a genuine tracking-gap closure with an unclosed current window — a clean read needs both effects to settle.
