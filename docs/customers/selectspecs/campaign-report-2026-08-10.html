Data Freshness & Prior-Report Caveat

The Aug 1–10 window is not closed

Generated today, Aug 10, 2026. The review table's MAX(ts_event) is Aug 9 — no Aug 10 reviews have landed yet — and invites sent late in the August window (Aug 5–10) have had only 0–5 days to convert vs 10–20 days for the July cohort. Expect August's rate to climb further as the window matures.

This calls the Jul 22 report's headline rate into question

The Jul 22 report's 6.26% (Jul 1–17) and 3.05% (Jun 1–17) "review rate" figures used the same same-window counting approach, without verifying via order_id that reviews actually came from invited orders — see Notes for why those numbers are likely overstated. Recommend re-running that comparison with proper order_id-based attribution before citing those percentages again.

Key Findings

Total review rate is up 50% and isn't affected by the tracking gap

Because it doesn't require order_id attribution, total review rate (4.40% → 6.60%) is the one number in this report unaffected by the order_id tracking gap (see Notes) — it's a straight count of all reviews landing in the window divided by invites sent, the same formula used in the Jul 22 report's benchmarks. Aug 1–10's 6.60% sits right in the 6%+ steady-state band that report identified.

August looks better even after accounting for the tracking gap

The as-observed jump in verified conversion rate (0.33% → 3.94%) is not a reliable read of behavioural change — most of it is the order_id gap closing (see Notes for a gap-adjusted estimate). Even under a generous adjustment, August still comes out ahead, so the direction of improvement is likely real; only the magnitude is uncertain.

Carryover matters — invites don't convert same-window

In August, carryover reviews (invited before the window, reviewed within it) are 21.2% of all reviews — nearly as large as the same-window-attributed share was for the entire July window. This confirms the account's known pattern of multi-week review lag and is why same-window-only rates will always understate a campaign's eventual yield.

Organic volume swung hard the other way

Using the consistent, combined definition (confirmed organic + no-join-key) for both periods, organic reviews fell from 70 (87.5% of all reviews) in Jul 22–31 to 20 (19.2%) in Aug 1–10 — a swing of the same size as the invite-attributed increase, and largely the mirror image of the tracking-gap finding above rather than a separate signal. Read the mix shift (organic-dominated → invite-dominated), not the organic count in isolation, since a chunk of what's now counted as "invite-attributed" in August would very likely have been counted the same way in July had the order_id been captured.

Rating quality improved

Average rating across all reviews (regardless of attribution) rose from 4.36★ to 4.46★. Sample sizes are small (80–104 reviews per window), so treat this as directional.

Notes — Methodology & Data Quality Detail

Definitions

Each review's order_id is joined against the full invite history (all-time, not just the window) to classify it as same-window (invited within the window being measured), carryover (invited earlier, reviewed in this window), confirmed organic (has an order_id, never invited), or no join key (no order_id/email — unattributable). Verified invitation conversion rate = same-window attributed ÷ invites sent that window. Total review rate = all reviews (any bucket) ÷ invites sent that window — not attribution-dependent. Organic — total combines confirmed organic + no join key as a best estimate.

Order_id tracking gap

77.5% of Jul 22–31 reviews (62/80) had no order_id or email at all, vs. 14.4% in Aug 1–10 (15/104) — a sharp change right at the month boundary (Jul 25–31: 77.8% missing; Aug 1: 35.7%; Aug 2–9: 11.1%), consistent with a data-pipeline fix landing ~Aug 1–2. This means the two windows aren't apples-to-apples on raw counts — a chunk of August's higher attributed-review share is newly-visible tracking, not necessarily new behaviour. Gap-adjusted estimate: applying the attribution split seen in July's 18 order_id-having reviews (33% same-window / 22% carryover / 44% organic) to its 62 unkeyed reviews yields an estimated verified conversion rate of ~1.47% for July, still below August's 3.94% — direction holds, magnitude uncertain.

Implication for the Jul 22 report

That report's 6.26% (Jul 1–17) and 3.05% (Jun 1–17) "review rate" figures used the same same-window-count method without an order_id join. Given the tracking gap found here likely extends into that period, those figures are probably overstated as a measure of true conversion — recommend re-running with proper attribution before citing them again.
