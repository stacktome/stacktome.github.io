Key Findings

Lower volume, flat quality

Invites fell 13.6% (6,253→5,404) and reviews fell 16.4% (342→286), so Review Rate (Total) eased to 5.29% from 5.47%. The average rating is unchanged at 4.51★; the 5-star share eased to 77.3% (from 80.1%) while the 1-star share fell to 5.6% (from 6.1%).

More reviews can be traced to their order

The Attribution Rate rose to 77.3% from 63.5%: 221 of 286 reviews followed an email about their own order, and the share of reviews carrying an order number rose to 81.8% (from 69.9%). The 65 unmatched reviews average 4.03★ against 4.65★ for attributed ones, and are not necessarily unprompted (52 have no order number).

Refund handling and prescriptions now stand out

Of 24 negative reviews, 13 (54%) concern customer service, refunds or pricing and 8 (33%) concern lenses or prescriptions (up from 15%), while delivery fell to 33% (from 48%). Only 3 of the 10 traceable negative reviewers have a ticket on file, and 58% of negatives cannot be traced at all because they carry no email.

Recommended Actions

  • 1
    Resolve the open cases and refund disputes first. Orders 4187250, 4185456, 3966468 and 4081560 and the disputed wrong-prescription refund are visible in public reviews. Several reviewers describe refunds that came back short (half refunded) or a return process that left them without proof of postage. Two related steps: resolve open complaints proactively (5 of the 24 negatives are customers still waiting for or chasing an order), and keep invitation timing consistent and based on delivery, so every customer is asked at the same point after their order arrives rather than before it.
  • 2
    Review how refunds, returns and the money-back guarantee are explained and handled. 13 of 24 negatives cite customer service, refunds or pricing, including a reviewer who bought two pairs without realising they overlapped and then found the 30-day guarantee hard to use, and another surprised by a changed promotional price. Clearer guarantee wording and a visible total before checkout would address several of these.
  • 3
    Check prescription accuracy and lens quality. Eight negatives (33%, from 15%) include wrong or upside-down prescriptions, two pairs with different prescriptions arriving identical, a very narrow varifocal field and weak transition lenses. This is the second spike in three months (August 41%, September 15%); worth raising a glazing/QC check with the optical lab if November shows the same.
  • 4
    Frame durability and warranty repairs. Six negatives (25%) mention thin side arms, loose nose pads or arms breaking within months. Offering a loan or replacement pair during repairs would address the most common frustration in these reviews.
  • 5
    Keep linking reviews to orders. 81.8% of October reviews carry an order number (September 69.9%); the remaining 52 reviews without one cannot be attributed to an invitation. Support-ticket matching works separately, using the reviewer’s email address rather than the order number; 14 of the 24 negative reviews carry no email, so no ticket can be matched for them. Continuing to improve order-number coverage will raise the Attribution Rate, and capturing the reviewer’s email would make support follow-up possible.
