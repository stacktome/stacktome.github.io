ITOL Recruit: review-invitation cooldown analysis

INTERNAL: for Evaldas, not for the customer  Window: requests received Oct 14 – Nov 12, 2026 (30 days) · Account itol_recruit · Prepared Nov 13, 2026 · Read-only analysis, no settings changed
Recommendation: change the cooldown to 7 days on Support (647) and General Recruitment Support (701) only. Leave every other campaign at 30 days.
  • Estimated impact of the recommended change: +10 invitations to 10 students in the 30-day window with a 7-day cooldown (104 → 114, +9.6%). A 4-day cooldown would give +13 invitations to 12 students (+12.5%). The difference between the two is 3 invitations a month.
  • Why 7 rather than 4: the 3 additional invitations at 4 days are the ones that land soonest after the previous emails. With 4 days a student can get invite, reminder, invite, reminder inside 10 days, and one student in the window (Selena's first example) would have received 3 Support invitations in just over 3 weeks. With 7 days nobody gets more than 2, and consecutive invitations are at least 10 days apart when a reminder was sent.
  • Why only these two campaigns: this is where the cooldown blocks genuinely new interactions. 70% of all cooldown suppressions are in Welcome Call/Setup (646), where a second form arrives within 1–3 days; those are correctly suppressed and would stay suppressed at 4 or 7 days anyway. Figures for applying the change to all campaigns are in Section 4 for comparison only.
  • Expected review effect is small: roughly one extra review a month at the account's recent 6–8% review rate.
  • Confirm one thing with engineering first: students who reviewed in the last 30 days are currently not invited. Two of Selena's three examples are in that position. If that exclusion is separate from the cooldown, the change will not release their requests; if it shortens with the cooldown, the impact rises to about +12 (7 days) or +15 (4 days) and recent reviewers will be invited again.

How suppression was determined

  • Suppression is not logged. The event store only holds sent emails (/events/invites/email); there is no skipped/suppressed event, and the campaign report API returns send, open and click statistics only. Every suppression figure here is therefore an estimate: a request event with no invitation, attributed to a cause by replaying the eligibility rules against the send history.
  • Request event = a row in the purchase feed. Form-triggered campaigns (633, 646–651, 698–701, 710) receive one event per staff form submission, routed by campaign id. Hubspot (Main) 624 receives deal events. Invitations go out a median of 56 minutes after the event.
  • The replay reproduces reality. Run with the current 30-day setting, the model predicts exactly the invitations that were actually sent (0 added, 0 missed out of 636 events), and all 178 unsent form requests are explained. No request was left as "unknown".

For comparison, applying the change to all form campaigns: 5 students with 2 same-campaign invitations at 7 days; 7 with 2 and 1 with 3 at 4 days; 15 and 17 students with 6 or more emails.

  • 7 days: no. At most two invitations per campaign in 30 days, never closer than 10 days when a reminder was sent, and 4 students affected.
  • 4 days: borderline. The volume is still small, but the pattern is poor for frequent contacts: invitation, reminder on day 3, new invitation from day 7, reminder on day 10. A student who contacts support weekly would be asked for a review every week. Selena's first example (Section 6) would have received three Support invitations and three reminders between Oct 21 and Nov 15.
  • Email load is already driven by cross-campaign sends, not by the cooldown. Because cooldown is per campaign, 170 students already received 4 or more emails in the window (typically Hubspot invite + reminder and Welcome Call invite + reminder in the same week). Shortening the cooldown on two campaigns adds little to this.
  • Students who already reviewed are protected only by the 30-day existing-review exclusion. 8 of the 23 affected support students have reviewed at some point. Of the extra invitations, 1 (7-day) or 2 (4-day) would go to a student with an older review. If the exclusion shortens with the cooldown, students could be invited again 7 days after reviewing.

Limits of this analysis

  • All suppression and scenario figures are estimates from event and send history, as suppression is not logged. They are consistent with the data (the 30-day replay matches actual sends exactly) but the platform's exact evaluation order is inferred.
  • "Support-related" was read as the staff-form-triggered campaigns, with Support (647) and General Recruitment Support (701) shown separately. If the customer means only 647, use its own row: 33 suppressed requests, 22 students, +9 (7 days) or +12 (4 days).
  • Volumes are small. A 30-day window gives 10–13 extra invitations for the recommended change; treat the per-campaign splits outside 647 as indicative.
  • Requests and sends were loaded from July 1. The cooldown and review rules were validated on data from Sep 20 onward, when they behave consistently.
  • Students are matched by email address. Invite rows were de-duplicated, as this account's invite files contain exact duplicate rows.
  • Review impact was not modelled beyond the rough rate applied in the recommendation.

Other things noticed (internal)

  • CV Redraft (710) can never send. Its segment (1137) filters on campaign id 697, while the form sends 710. All 6 requests since July went unsent; there were none in the window.
  • Hubspot (Main) 624 depends on a race. 63 of its 213 invitations went to students whose form request arrived up to 40 minutes earlier, before the segment refreshed. These students usually receive the form-campaign invitation as well.
  • Duplicate form submissions. Several forms arrive as multiple identical events in the same minute (up to 11 for one student). They are harmless today because the cooldown absorbs them, and remain blocked in both scenarios.
  • If the cooldown is changed, it is a per-campaign setting, so it can be limited to 647 and 701. The A/B template test that started Nov 6 is running on the same campaigns; a cooldown change would slightly alter the audience mix (more repeat contacts) part-way through the test.
