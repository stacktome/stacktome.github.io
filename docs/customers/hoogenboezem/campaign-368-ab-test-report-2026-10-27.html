2. Test Setup

From September 29, both steps of the Trustpilot campaign each ran two template versions side by side, with a 50/50 target split. The invitation test and the reminder test are independent. Each invitation recipient was randomly assigned an invitation version, and each reminder recipient was separately assigned a reminder version.

Significance is assessed with a standard test for small samples (Fisher’s exact test, 95% confidence). With about 50 sends per version, only large gaps can be confirmed. To confirm a click-rate gap like the one seen in the invitations (about 19% vs 11%), roughly 250–270 sends per version would be needed, which is about 5 months at the current sending volume. Confirming differences in review rate would need considerably more.

Looking beyond the test, overall Trustpilot sentiment in the period is strong: an average of 4.46★, with 12 of 13 reviews at 4–5★. Neither version produced any negative review. The only 1★ review was organic and unrelated to the campaign emails.

8. Recommendations

1. Use the original reminder as the standard reminder

Its click-through advantage is large, consistent across every send day, and statistically reliable. Consider sending it to 100% of reminder recipients, or shift to an 80/20 split in its favour if you would like to keep monitoring the Trustpilot-style version.

2. Keep the invitation test running, leaning towards the original

The original invitation is ahead on clicks but not yet proven. Either keep the 50/50 split and review again in a few months, or move to a 70/30 split favouring the original. That keeps most traffic on the better-performing version while still collecting comparison data.

3. Test one element at a time next

The original versions combine a personal, formal tone, a named sender (Doris) and the €100 wooncheque draw. The Trustpilot-style versions have none of these. A follow-up test changing a single element, for example adding the prize-draw line to the Trustpilot-style email, would show which element is driving the extra clicks.

4. Judge future tests on reviews once volume allows

Clicks are the best available signal today, but reviews are the real goal. 5 of the 13 Trustpilot reviews received this period could not be confirmed as campaign or organic, and Trustpilot shows even the campaign reviews as “without invitation”. Inviting customers through order-linked Trustpilot invitation links would connect each review to its order and show it on Trustpilot as an invited review. Future tests could then compare review rates directly, not just clicks.

5. Consider a subject-line test after the template decision

Both steps currently use one subject line each. Once the template choice is settled, testing a second subject line, particularly for the invitation (42.3% open rate), is a low-effort way to lift opens.

6. Keep delivery expectations clear

Delivery and assembly crews are the strongest driver of positive reviews, and delivery timing is the main improvement point among the 4★ reviews. Proactive updates when a delivery is delayed or running late could protect this. The 1★ damage case is already being handled through the company reply.
