Rating: essentially unchanged. 4.36★ (Jan–May) vs 4.29★ (Jun–Aug) is a 0.07★ difference, and the 5-star share is flat (72.9% vs 72.1%). Whatever changed in the review pipeline between these two periods, it is not showing up as a change in how customers rate their experience.

Review volume: 264/month (Jan–May) vs 144/month (Jun–Aug). This is not linked to fewer invites going out — invites sent per month stayed level (10,096/mo → 10,549/mo, +4.5%) — and total order volume was steady across the whole window (roughly 12k–18k orders/month, see Section 2). So the change in review count tracks with something in the review pipeline rather than with fewer customers or fewer invites.

Attribution: 93.7% of reviews carried a matchable order_id in Jan–May, vs 18.8% in Jun–Aug. Since campaign attribution runs on a straight join against order_id, this field is what explains most of the attributed-vs-organic shift, and plausibly a share of the volume change too. Section 3 has the month-by-month detail and timing.

Between May and June, invites sent increased (8,074 → 10,901) while order_id coverage on incoming reviews went from 91.1% to 3.1%, and review count went from 192 to 127. March through May each sat in the 91–97% order_id coverage range; June through August have sat in the 3–37% range. That timing — a change concentrated right at the May/June boundary rather than building up gradually across the prior months — is more consistent with a change to a data field or integration than with a gradual shift in customer behavior. Rating in June (4.46★) was in fact the second-highest of the whole window, reinforcing that this isn't a satisfaction-driven change.

order_id coverage has been recovering since: 3.1% (Jun) → 15.2% (Jul) → 37.4% (Aug). Worth checking with engineering/Trustpilot whether a fix has already been applied, and continuing to track this monthly — if it keeps climbing back toward the ~90%+ baseline seen Jan–May, review rate should follow it back up.

6. Recommended Actions

PriorityAreaAction
High Trustpilot integration order_id coverage on incoming reviews went from ~93% to ~3% between May and June, and has recovered to ~37% by August. Worth confirming with engineering/Trustpilot whether an API, webhook payload, or invite-link parameter changed around end of May — this is the most direct lever on both attribution and, likely, part of the review-count change, and rating is not at risk either way.
High Review volume Monthly review count moved from 264/mo to 144/mo across the two periods, without a matching drop in invites sent or order volume. Independent of the root-cause fix above, this is worth tracking since fewer reviews means slower growth of the public review count on Trustpilot — not a satisfaction concern, but a visibility one.
Medium Customer service / in-store The most-mentioned theme in negative reviews across the year (34.8%) — slow refund follow-up and inconsistent in-store warranty/complaint handling recur in both periods. A consistent escalation path for warranty disputes across branches would address the specific Aarhus case above and similar ones.
Medium Delivery timing / tracking Second most-mentioned theme (30.4%) — delayed dispatch and stale tracking status, several tied to a specific date the customer needed the item by. Coordinate with fulfillment/logistics on dispatch-time and tracking-update gaps.
Medium Stock accuracy 26.9% of negative reviews mention items shown as available at checkout that later turn out unavailable, wrong-variant, or cancelled outright. Points to inventory-sync lag between storefront and warehouse.
Monitor Recovery trend order_id coverage: 3.1% (Jun) → 15.2% (Jul) → 37.4% (Aug). If this keeps climbing toward the Jan–May baseline, review rate should follow without further intervention — worth a monthly check-in.
Monitor Returns QC A small number of reports (product quality theme, 8.4% of negatives) describe visibly used items shipped as new — worth a quick look at returns-restocking inspection with the warehouse.
