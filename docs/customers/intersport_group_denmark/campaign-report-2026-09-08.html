Bottom line: review volume fell ~46% (monthly average), and it is not because fewer invites went out. Invites sent per month were essentially flat (10,096/mo Jan–May vs 10,549/mo Jun–Aug, +4.5%) and total order volume stayed in its normal 12k–18k/month band throughout — Intersport's business volume did not change. The review pipeline did.

Root cause: order_id stopped arriving on most incoming Trustpilot reviews starting June 1, 2026. 93.7% of Jan–May reviews carried a matchable order reference (which is how invites get credited); in June that collapsed to 3.1%, recovering only partially to 37.4% by August. Since attribution is a straight join on order_id, this single field is what is driving both the attribution collapse (93.7% → 15.3% invite-linked) and, most likely, part of the raw volume drop — reviews that fail silently in the invite→Trustpilot→webhook round trip when the reference can't be carried through never make it back to us at all. This lines up precisely with a Trustpilot-side API/webhook change, not a demand or satisfaction issue: average rating barely moved (4.36★ → 4.29★).

3. Monthly Trend & Root Cause

Invites held steady or grew every month; reviews and the share of reviews carrying an order_id both fell off a cliff between May and June, then partially recovered in August.

The break is a cliff, not a slope. May → June: invites sent actually rose (8,074 → 10,901, +35%) while order_id presence on reviews collapsed (91.1% → 3.1%) and review count nearly halved (192 → 127). Every month from March through May sat at 91–97% order_id coverage; every month from June onward sat at 3–37%. That step-change, precisely at the May/June boundary, is the signature of an integration change on Trustpilot's side rather than a gradual organic decline.

August is trending back up (37.4% order_id coverage vs 3.1% in June, review rate recovering from 1.17% to 1.29%) — worth confirming with engineering whether a partial fix has already landed, and tracking whether it continues climbing back toward the ~95% pre-change baseline.

Email delivery and engagement look healthy — 62.2% opens and 2.5% CTR are solid for a review-invite flow, and unsubscribes (105 of 11,551 sent, 0.9%) are unremarkable. This corroborates the root-cause read: customers are opening the invite and clicking through at a normal rate, but the resulting Trustpilot review is coming back to us without the order reference needed to attribute it — and a share of them appear not to be landing in our review table at all. The problem is downstream of the email, in the invite-link → Trustpilot → webhook round trip.
